Reading the balance

New Providence now carries enough nameplate generation for peak — the binding constraint has shifted to the distribution network: feeder and substation bottlenecks overload and trip under high demand, which is what turned a single lightning strike at Blue Hills (Jul 5 '26) into an island-wide blackout. Bahamas Grid Company's rebuild (underway since Apr '25) isn't expected to be felt before summer 2027. Family Island grids are the opposite problem: thin, single-station systems where one engine failure equals an island outage.

Where renewables land

Committed build-out: three utility-scale PV farms on New Providence (CV Bethel, Coral Harbour, Baillou Hills) each paired with BESS; ~24 Family Island projects under PPA — Family Island Microgrid Co. on Harbour Is., Bimini, Moore's Is., Black Point, Staniel Cay, Farmer's Cay; RenugenPro solar+BESS+LNG on San Salvador, Long Island, Cat Island. Ragged Island already runs BPL's model solar microgrid. Government target: 128 MW PV, 30% RE by 2030.

How this dispatches
  • Solar and wind serve load first; surplus charges the battery, the rest is curtailed.
  • Battery discharges into any remaining net load before thermal starts (88% round-trip).
  • Off-peak (load below 72% of the day's peak) the battery also tops up from thermal headroom, which is how island storage actually runs. Note what that does: with no renewable surplus to soak, a battery raises total fuel slightly through round-trip losses while cutting the peak thermal call. Its value there is firm capacity, reserve and inertia — not fuel. Fuel savings only arrive once solar or wind is large enough to spill.
  • Thermal fills the balance up to firm capacity; anything left is unserved.
  • Load shape is a tropical evening-peaking curve; solar is a clear-sky bell scaled to a 21% annual capacity factor at "Typical".
  • Screening dispatch — no unit commitment, no minimum loads, no reserve constraint, no network limits. 8760-hour modelling is required for design.
